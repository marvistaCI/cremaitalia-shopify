# The Crema Italia taste quiz, and what we ask roasters for

Written 2026-11-24 for Lucia Calo, so she can explain to roasters what we will ask them to
provide for each coffee and why. Decided with Steve the same day. This describes the intended
quiz; the storefront change is being built the same day.

## What the quiz is

When a new visitor arrives at cremaitalia.com we offer a short quiz, three questions, that helps
them find coffees they are likely to enjoy. Their answers filter our catalog. The quiz is
optional and can be skipped at any point.

**Question 1. How do you like your roast?**

- Light roast: brighter and lighter in body, with a lively acidity.
- Medium roast: balanced, rounded, the classic bar espresso.
- Dark roast: fuller body, low acidity, the roast leads.
- Surprise me: no preference.

**Question 2. Which tasting notes appeal to you most?**

- Fruit and flowers: jasmine, black tea, peach, raspberry, orange.
- Sweet and chocolatey: caramelized, honey, hazelnut, almond, chocolate.
- Dark and spiced: pipe tobacco, cinnamon, clove, raisin, dark chocolate.
- Not sure yet: skip this one.

**Question 3. Any caffeine considerations?**

- Full caffeine.
- Decaf.
- Both, show me everything.

## How it aligns with the SCA

The Specialty Coffee Association, with World Coffee Research, publishes the Coffee Taster's
Flavor Wheel. It is the standard vocabulary for describing coffee flavor, and the SCA's current
cupping standard (SCA 103, Descriptive Assessment) uses it as a check-all-that-apply list: the
cupper ticks the wheel categories that apply to the cup.

Our quiz uses the same vocabulary, in two ways.

1. Every flavor word a customer sees in Question 2 is a term from the Flavor Wheel. Our three
   choices are simply three groups of wheel categories, collected so a non-expert can choose
   between them:
   - Fruit and flowers collects the wheel's Floral and Fruity categories (including black tea,
     berries, citrus and other fruit) and the pleasant fermented notes such as winey.
   - Sweet and chocolatey collects Sweet (brown sugar, caramelized, honey, vanilla) and
     Nutty/Cocoa (hazelnut, almond, chocolate).
   - Dark and spiced collects Roasted (pipe tobacco, brown roast, grain, malt), Spices
     (cinnamon, clove, nutmeg, anise, pepper) and Dried Fruit (raisin, prune).
2. Roast level is not on the Flavor Wheel, so Question 1 does not use flavor words at all. It
   describes roast through the attributes the SCA scores separately, acidity and body. This keeps
   the two questions from repeating each other.

Defect terms on the wheel (ashy, acrid, musty, papery, vegetative and so on) are not offered to
customers and are not on the roaster's list.

## What we ask each roaster for, and how it feeds the quiz

For every coffee we list, we ask the roaster for three things. They describe the coffee as they
cup it, in the vocabulary they already know. They never see our three customer groups; the
grouping is ours, so if we later adjust how the groups are drawn, nothing needs to be asked of
the roaster again.

**1. Roast degree.** One of light, medium or dark. If the roaster measures roast colour (an
Agtron or similar reading), we would rather have the number, and we will place it in the right
band ourselves.

**2. Flavor descriptors.** From a closed list of Flavor Wheel terms, tick every one that applies,
up to five, the same limit the SCA form uses. The list we offer:

- Floral: jasmine, rose, chamomile, black tea
- Fruity: blackberry, raspberry, blueberry, strawberry, cherry, peach, pear, apple, grape,
  pomegranate, pineapple, coconut, orange, lemon, lime, grapefruit, winey
- Dried fruit: raisin, prune
- Sweet: caramelized, brown sugar, molasses, maple syrup, honey, vanilla
- Nutty and cocoa: hazelnut, almond, peanut, chocolate, dark chocolate
- Spices: cinnamon, clove, nutmeg, anise, pepper
- Roasted: pipe tobacco, tobacco, brown roast, smoky, grain, malt

The SCA publishes the wheel in translation; we will supply the list in Italian against the
official terms, not our own translation, once we have confirmed the Italian edition.

**3. Caffeine.** Regular or decaffeinated.

How it ties together: a roaster tells us a blend is medium roast and ticks hazelnut, brown
sugar, dark chocolate and cinnamon. On our side that coffee is placed in Sweet and chocolatey
(hazelnut, brown sugar, dark chocolate) and in Dark and spiced (dark chocolate, cinnamon). A
customer who answers Medium roast and Sweet and chocolatey sees it. A customer who answers Light
roast and Fruit and flowers does not. A coffee can belong to more than one group, and most will.

For a Sorpresa collection (a box of several coffees), the box takes on the roast levels and
flavor groups of every coffee inside it, so nothing extra is asked for the box itself.

## Why this matters to roasters

The descriptors do two jobs. They drive the quiz and the filters, and they appear on the coffee's
page as its tasting notes. So the roaster is describing their own coffee to the customer, in
standard terms, and the words they choose are the words that will be shown.
